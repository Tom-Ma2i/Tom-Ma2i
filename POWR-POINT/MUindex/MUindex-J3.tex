\noindent
For measuring an individual's voting power of a voting game,
   some power indices are proposed.
In this paper,
  we propose a new power index which is useful 
    in situations that not all coalitions are 
    equally likely to be formed.  
 Our power index includes the ordinary Shapley-Shubic index, 
  the Deegan-Packel index  and the Curiel index as special cases.
 

\medskip

\noindent
{\bf Key Words}: voting game, simple game, 
    weighted majority game,
    power index.

\section{Introdution}


 本稿では，新たな非対称投票力指数の提案を行う．
 提案する指数が他の多くの非対称投票力指数と異なる点は，
イデオロギー空間の存在を仮定する必要が無い事である．
 さらに，提案する指数は，その特殊ケースとして
 Shapley-Shubic 指数と Deegan-Packel 指数を含んでいる．
 本稿では，提案した指数を与える公理系についても記す．
 最後に提案した指数を，1997年の Ono and Muto で用いられた，
  1989から1992の間の参議院のデータに適用した結果を示す．

 投票システムにおいて，各投票者の持つ力を測るために，
  Shapley-Shubic 指数 \cite{SHAPLEY1954}, 
  Banzhaf 指数 \cite{BANZHAF},  
  Deegan-Packel 指数 \cite{DEEGAN1978} 等， 
 様々な投票力指数が提案されている．
  政治的な投票システムでは，各投票者は政治的な信条を持っており，
 それぞれが提携を組む可能性は対称的ではなく，
 政治的な信条に依存して非対称的である．
  このような状況での投票者の力を測る指数として，
  Owen \cite{OWEN1971} と Shapley \cite{SHAPLEY1977}
  はイデオロギー空間を用いた非対称投票力指数の提案を行っている．
 Ono and Muto は， 論文 \cite{ONO1997}において，
  非対称 Shapley-Owen 指数を日本の参議院データに適用した場合，
   得られる結果が現実と非常に異なる事を指摘した．
  更に，Shapley-Owen 指数における議案の発生の均等性の仮定を外し，
   代わりに現実の議案の
   賛成／反対のデータを用いて各会派の投票力指数の計算を行った．

\section{投票力指数の定義}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% def of voting game %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 
 任意の集合$S$とその要素 $i$ に対し,
 集合 $S \cup \{i\}$ を $S+i$と書き，
  $S \setminus \{i\}$ を $S-i$と書く. 
 投票者の集合を $N=\{1, 2, \ldots , n\}$とする．
 投票者の部分集合を提携と呼ぶ．
 投票ゲームは $G=(N, \calW)$ と表される，
 ただし $\calW$は$N$を服務提携族 
 $N \in \calW \subseteq 2^N$ であり，   
 $\forall E \in \calW$,  
 $\forall F \supseteq E$, $F \in \calW$
 を満たすものとする.
提携族 $\calW$ 中の提携は勝利提携と呼ばれ，
 勝利提携でない提携は敗北提携と呼ばれる．
ある勝利提携$E$が，その真部分集合として勝利提携を持たないならば，
 $E$は極小勝利提携であると呼ばれる．

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% characteristic function %%%%%%%%%%%%%

 投票ゲーム$G$の特性関数
  $v_G: 2^N \rightarrow \{0, 1\}$ は以下のように定義される;
\[ 
 v_G(E)=\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 & (E \mbox{は勝利提携} ),\\
                                  0 & (E \mbox{は敗北提携} ).
                \end{array} \right.
\] 
 混乱のおそれが無いときは， 特性関数を単に $v$ と書く．

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% profile %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% 

 投票ゲーム $G=(N, \calW)$ のプロフィール $p$とは，
  $\calW$  上に定義された非負関数
  $p:\calW \rightarrow  [0,1]$ で，その総和が1，
  すなわち $\sum_{E \in \calW}p(E)=1$を満たすものである.
 与えられた投票ゲーム$G$の勝利提携$E$に対し，
  単純プロフィール $p_E \in \{0, 1\}^{\calW}$とは，
  $G$のプロファイルで
  $Ｅ=Ｆ$ ならばその時に限り$p_E(F)=1$となるものを言う．
 
%%%%%%%%%%%% subgames %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 
 投票ゲーム $G=(N, \calW)$とその勝利提携 $E \in \calW$ に対し,  
  $\calW [E]=\{F subseteq N  \mid  F \cap E \in \calW \}$
 とし，  投票ゲーム$(N,  \calW [E])$を$G[E]$と表す，
  本稿では，投票ゲーム$G[E]$の
  Shapley-Shubic 指数 \cite{SHAPLEY1954} を
  $\phi [E] \in [0, 1]^N$と表す. 

%%%%%%%%%%%%%% def of eta index %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% 

 では，本稿で提案する投票力指数 である $\eta$指数 を定義しよう．
 投票ゲーム $G=(N, \calW)$ とそのプロフィール $p$ が与えられたとき，
 $\eta$指数  $\eta (G, p) \in [0, 1]^N$ 
 は以下のように定義される；
\[
      \eta (G, p) = \sum_{E \in \calW} p(E) \phi [E].  
 \]


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% S-S index and D-P index %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%


 The $\eta$-index includes  the Shapley-Shubic index and
    the Deegan-Packel index as a special case.
 When the profile is the special simple profile $p_N$,
   then the $\eta$-index is equivalent 
  to the ordianry Shapley-Shubic index.
 If the given profile is defined by
\[
    p(E)= \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} 
      1/W & (E \mbox{ is a minimal winning coalition }), \\
      0   & ( \mbox{ otherwise }),
                  \end{array} \right.
\]
   where $W$ denotes the number of minimal winning coalitions,
   then the $\eta$-index is equivalent 
   to the Deegan-Packel index.
 It is because, 
   for any minimal winning coalition $E$ 
   of the given voting game $G$,
   the Shaley-Shubic index $\phi$ 
   of the voting game $G[E]$  satisfies
\[
 \phi _i = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} 1/|E| & (i \in E) \\
                                        0  & (i \not \in E).  
                   \end{array} \right.
\]

\section{Axiomatic Characterization}

In the following, 
  we introduce axioms which implies the $\eta$-index.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% axioms %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\medskip \noindent
(Axiom 1)  Let $G=(N, \calW)$ be a voting game and 
  $p_E$ a simple profile satisfying $E \in \calW$.
 If a player $i \in E$ satifies that
    $\forall F \subseteq N -i$, $v(F)=v(F+i)$,
   then $ \eta (G, p)_i = 0$. 

\medskip \noindent
(Axiom 2) Let $G=(N, \calW)$ be a voting game and 
  $p_E$ a simple profile satisfying $E \in \calW$.
 If a pair of players $i, j \in E$ satisfies that
   $v(F+i)=v(F+j)$ for all $F \subseteq N \setminus \{i, j\}$, 
  then $\eta (G, p_E)_i= \eta (G, p_E)_j$. 

\medskip \noindent 
(Axiom 3) For any pair of voting games
    $G_1=(N, \calW _1)$, $G_2=(N, \calW _2)$,
  and a simple profile $p_E$ satisfying 
  $E \in \calW _1 \cap \calW _2$,
     $\eta (G_1, p_E) + \eta (G_2, p_E)
   = \eta (G_{\wedge},  p_E) + \eta (G_{\vee},  p_E)$ 
  where $G_{\wedge}=(N, \calW _1 \cup \calW _2)$ 
   and  $G_{\vee}=(N, \calW _1 \cap \calW _2)$. 

\medskip \noindent
(Axiom 4)  Let $G=(N, \calW)$ be a voting game and 
  $p_E$ a simple profile satisfying $E \in \calW$.
 The sum total of $\eta$-index is equal to 1;
  i. e., $\sum_{i \in N} \eta (G, p_E)_i =1$.

\medskip \noindent
 (Axiom 5) For any voting game $G$ and a pair of profiles 
    $p_1$ and $p_2$ of $G$, 
  $\eta$-index satify the property that
  $\eta (G, \lambda p_1+ (1-\lambda)p_2)
     = \lambda \eta (G, p_1) + (1-\lambda) \eta (G, p_2)$
  for all $ \lambda \in [0, 1]$).

\medskip \noindent
(Axiom 6)  Let $G=(N, \calW)$ be a voting game and 
  $p_E$ a simple profile satisfying $E \in \calW$.
  Then $\eta (G, p_E) = \eta (G[E], p_E) $.
 
Axioms 1, 2, 3 and 4 are similar to axioms
  used in the definition of the Shapley-Shubic power index. 
If we fix the simple profile 
  used in Axioms 1, 2, 3 and 4 to $p_N$, 
  these axioms are equivalent to those used in the definition 
  of the Shapley-Shubic power index.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% theorem %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 
 We denote all the pairs of voting game 
  and its profile by $\calG$. 
 A functin $f : \calG \rightarrow \Real$ is called 
   a {\em power index}. 
 
\begin{theorem}
  A power index is the $\eta$-index
  if and only if the power index satisfies Axioms 1,$\ldots$, 6.
\end{theorem}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% 
\noindent
{\bf proof:} 
 It is easy to show that the $\eta$-index satisfies
   Axioms 1,$\ldots$, 6. 
 Next, we show that a power index $\eta: \calG \rightarrow [0, 1]$
  satisfying Axioms 1,$\ldots$, 6 
  is the $\eta$-index. 
 
 Let  $G=(N, \calW)$ be a voting game.
 First, we consider the simple profile $p_E$ for each $E \in \calW$.
 Axiom 6 implies that $\eta (G, p_E) = \eta (G[E], p_E)$ 
  and Axiom 1 implies that $\eta (G, p_E)_i =0$
  for each player $i \not \in E$.
 Then we can show that the $\eta$-index is the unique index
  which satisfies Axioms 1,$\ldots$, 6.
 The proof is the same with that for the uniqueness of 
  the ordinary Shaple-Shubic index
  with respect to the corresponding Axioms.
  
 Next, we consider a general profile $p$.
 The profile $p$ is a vector indexed by $\calW$ and so 
  we can denote the profile by
  the covex combination of unit vectors $p_E$ as follows; 
 \[  
     p = \sum_{E \in \calW} p(E)p_E. 
 \]
 Then Axiom 5 implies that 
   the $\eta$-index satisfies the equality that;
 \[
    \eta (G, p)=\sum_{E \in \calW}  p(E) \eta (G, p_E).
 \]
 We have shown above that when the given profile is simple,
  the $\eta$-index is unique power index
  satisfying Axioms 1,$\ldots$, 6.
Thus, for any (general) profile,
    the $\eta$-index is unique power index
    satisfying Axioms 1,$\ldots$, 6.
 \QED

\section{Dual Game}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% dual game %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% 
 Given a voting game $G=(N, \calW)$ 
  with losing coalitions $\calL$, 
   the dual game is the pair $G^*=(N, \calW ^*)$
   where $\calW^* 
   = \{E \subseteq N \mid N \setminus E \in \calL\}$. 
 It is clear that $G^*$ is also a voting game.
 A power index for the dual game indicates the power
  for blocking issues.
 It is well-known that 
  the Shapley-Shubic index satisfies that 
  $\phi (G)= \phi (G^*)$.
 In the following, 
  we discuss the power to block issues
  for the voting game with profile.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% profile %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% 
 Given a voting game $G=(N, \calW)$ 
   with the family of losing coalitions $\calL$,  
   a {\em losing profile} $q$ of  $G$ is
  a function $q:\calL \rightarrow  [0,1]$
  satisfying that $\sum_{E \in \calL}q(E)=1$.
%%%%%%%%%%%%%% def of eta* index %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% 
 For any losing coaltion $N \setminus E$, 
  we estimate the power of the coalition $E$ 
  for blocking issuues by the Shapley-Shubic index
  $\phi (G^* [E])$ 
  where $G^*[E]$ is the voting game defined by 
  the winning coalitions
  $\calW^* [E] = \{F \subseteq E  \mid F \cap E \in \calW^* \}$.
 Given a voting game $G=(N, \calW)$ 
  with losing coalitions $\calL$
  and a losing profile $q$ of $G$,
  the $\eta^*$-index  $\eta^* (G, q) \in [0, 1]^N$ 
  is defined as follows;
 \[
      \eta^* (G, q) = \sum_{N \setminus E \in \calL}
       q(N \setminus E) \phi (G^* [E]).  
 \]


%%%%%%%%%%% proper game %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 A voting game is called a {\em proper} game
  when every coalition $E \subset N$ satisfies that
  $v(E) + v  (N \setminus E) \leq 1$. 
 In many application settings, 
  the voting systems are proper games. 
 If every coalition $E \subseteq N$ satisfies 
  $v(E) + v (N \subseteq N) = 1$, 
  we say that the voting game is
  a {\em constant sum game}.
%%%%%%%%% dual profile %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 For any profile $p: \calW \rightarrow [0, 1]$,
  the function $p^*:E \mapsto p(N \setminus E)$
  is called the dual profile of $p$.
  When the given voting game is proper,
   the dual profile is a losing profile.
 It is easy to see that 
  there exists a proper voting game
  such that the equation
  $\eta (G, p) = \eta^* (G, p^*)$ does not hold.
 If the given voting game $G$ is a constant sum game,
  we can show that the equality 
  $\eta (G, p)=\eta^* (G, p^*)$ holds
  for any profile $p$ of $G$.

  in the the House of Councilors in Japan in their paper 
 \cite{ONO1997}.

 using the real profile data from 1989 to 1992.

\section{Discussions}

 In this paper, we proposed a new power index 
  for oting game.
 We showed that
   a set of axioms defines our power index.
 We also studied political party power 
  in the House of Councilors in Japan.

 In \cite{ONO1997},
  Ono and Muto proposed that
  a direct application of the nonsymmetric Shapley-Owen index 
  gives a result contradicting ordinary outstanding
 They concluded that 
  the assumption of the random appearance of possible issues
  is not reasonable in this case.
 It is because, the bills were usually submitted by a cabinet
  consisting mainly of members belonging to the largest party.
 They also proposed a method which uses the factor analysis
  to determine the position of parties 
  in the ideological space and 
  represents the biased distribution of bills by
  the observed pattrens of yea/nay combination
  and its frequencies. 
 Conparing to Ono and Muto's method,
  Owen's original method 
  places both parties and issues in the same space, 
 
 The main difference of our index from 
   the nonsymmetric Shapley-Owen index 
  and Ono and Muto's method is that 
   we do not need to assume the existence of ideological space.
 We measure the ideological difference among parties by 
  the patterns of yea/nay combinations.  
 A bias of the distribution of patterns 
   defines the ideological distances among parties. 
 It means that we do not need to identify 
  the absolute position of each party
  in the ideological space 
  and so the obtained result based on 
  the relative difference among parties.
 From the methodological point of view,  
  our index mesures the difference among parties  
  directly from the biased distribution of observed issues
  without using factor analysis.
 
 
 As described in \cite{ONO1997}
  the frequency of submitted issues 
   might be influenced by the prenegotiations.
 Similarly to the existing power indices based on 
  submitted issues, 
  our index also could not cover the influece to the issues
  withdrawn without submitting.
